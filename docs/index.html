claude-automations

Three personal automations that run on my Mac every day. I built them by directing Claude Code; macOS schedules them, plain scripts do everything that must be exact, and Claude makes the calls that need judgment.

Three automations

Each one ends in a styled email. If anything fails, I get an alert with the end of the log instead of silence.

Daily · 7:58 AM
Morning briefing
  • Weather, alerts and a 3-day forecast from the National Weather Service
  • Sunrise, sunset and first/last light, calculated, so I'm not caught out after dark
  • Today's and tomorrow's calendar, read-only
  • No AI. Nothing here needs judgment, so nothing can be rate-limited or invent an event
Daily · 8:00 AM
Job search
  • A script sweeps ~280 company job boards through their public APIs
  • Filters title, location, required travel and age; dedupes against roles already seen and applied to
  • Claude reads my profile and the shortlist and decides keep or skip, with a reason
Mondays · 8:15 AM
Fuel prices
  • Claude fetches three stations' prices (the source blocks plain scripts)
  • The script validates them, keeps a history and flags anomalies
  • Savings per fill for two vehicles, and whether the detour is worth the drive

How it fits together

One pattern for every job. Claude is a single, narrow step with a strict contract, not the thing in charge.

→

Design choices

  1. Use AI only where it adds judgment. The briefing is pure code. Weather, sun times and calendar formatting don't need a model.
  2. Give the model a narrow job and a strict contract. Claude returns {"decisions": [{"url", "decision", "note"}]} for every candidate. Anything it misses stays in the log, marked unranked.
  3. Validate before acting. Fuel prices must be present and within a plausible range before anything is written or emailed.
  4. Always leave a result. The job search writes an unranked log before Claude runs and emails it even if the ranking fails.
  5. Fail loudly, to me only. One wrapper logs every run and emails an alert on any failure.
  6. Test through the real path. macOS grants calendar access per launching app, so every job was tested with a one-time scheduled run, not just from the terminal.

Bugs caught by testing

Every calculation and filter was checked against cases where I already knew the right answer. These are the ones that would otherwise have shipped.

How I worked with Claude Code

  1. Diagnose before fixing. Root causes with evidence (logs, transcripts, macOS permission logs) before any change.
  2. Known-answer tests. Rounding to the cent, daylight-saving days, filter edge cases.
  3. Small, reviewable changes, tested on copies first, with real emails only to me.
  4. Verify the outcome, not the report. The inbox and the logs, not "succeeded".
  5. Choose reliability over novelty. An earlier version ran on a self-hosted agent with free models; when two of those models were discontinued within two weeks, I moved the judgment steps to Claude and the scheduling to macOS.
